Compare & Contrast Essay Prewriting Workshop

This workshop helps you turn a topic, research, and your own ideas into a working compare-and-contrast essay outline. Your topic can be almost anything your teacher approves. Complete each section in your own words. Your responses save automatically on this device.

Assignment targets: unique title; clear thesis; at least 2 full pages; 2 outside sources; at least 3 relevant quotes with in-text citations and follow-up explanation; 3+ vocabulary words; strong topic and transition sentences; conclusion that connects the comparison back to the main idea; abstract; and Works Cited planning.

1. Choose & Understand Your Topic

2. Compare First: What Is Similar?

Do not worry about complete sentences yet. Brainstorm what the two subjects have in common.

3. Contrast Next: What Is Different?

4. Decide What the Comparison MEANS

A compare-and-contrast essay should do more than list similarities and differences. Decide what readers should understand after seeing the comparison.

5. Outside Source #1

Research Requirement: Your essay must use two outside sources. Source #1 should help you understand or compare one or both subjects.

Choose how you will introduce the source

6. Outside Source #2

Research Requirement: Use a second outside source so your comparison is supported by more than one source.

Choose how you will introduce the source

7. Plan Your Third Quote

Rubric Requirement: Plan at least 3 relevant quotes. Your third quote can come from either outside source. Every quote needs an in-text citation and your own explanation.

8. Vocabulary Planner

Rubric Requirement: Plan at least 3 vocabulary words and use them naturally in the essay.

9. Build Your Thesis

Your thesis should name both subjects and explain the main point of your comparison—not simply say that they are “similar and different.”

10. Create a Unique Working Title

Avoid titles such as “Compare and Contrast Essay.” Give the reader a clue about what you are comparing.

11. Plan the Introduction

Rubric Check: Your introduction should clearly identify both subjects, establish the main idea, and end with a clear thesis.

14. Optional Body Paragraph 3 — Another Key Comparison

Use this paragraph if you need another point to fully explain your comparison or reach the required two-page length.

15. Plan the Conclusion

Rubric Check: Do more than repeat the essay. Bring the comparison together and explain what readers should understand because of it.
